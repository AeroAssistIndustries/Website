"Where's a good brunch spot near me that's open now?" More people are asking questions like this to an AI assistant, or seeing an AI-written answer at the top of Google. Here's what that changes for local businesses, and what it doesn't.

What's actually changing

Search used to mean typing a few words and scanning a list of links. Now many people ask a full question and get a summarized answer: on Google, in AI chat assistants, and in the maps and voice assistants on their phones. Those answers often name a handful of businesses and explain why.

The assistant has to get its information from somewhere. It draws on what's published about you: your Google Business Profile, your website, reviews, directory listings and what other sites say about you.

What stays the same

The fundamentals of local marketing matter more, not less. AI tools look for clear, consistent and trustworthy information, which is exactly what good local SEO has always been about.

Five things to do now

  1. Keep your Google Business Profile complete and current. Categories, hours, services, attributes and photos all describe you in a way machines can read.
  2. Make your details match everywhere. Your name, address, phone number and hours should be the same on your website, Google, Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook and any directories you're listed in.
  3. Earn genuine, detailed reviews. Reviews that mention what you do ("best gluten-free bread in town", "fixed our AC the same day") help assistants understand what you're known for.
  4. Answer real questions on your website. Write plainly about what you offer, who it's for, where you are, what it costs and how to book. A good FAQ page helps humans and AI alike.
  5. Make your website easy to read for machines. Fast pages, clear headings and structured data (behind-the-scenes labels for your business details) help tools read your information correctly.

What not to do

Be wary of anyone promising to "rank you in ChatGPT" overnight. No one controls what AI assistants say. The honest approach is to make accurate, helpful information about your business easy to find, and to keep earning a good reputation.

Try it yourself

Ask a couple of AI assistants the questions your customers would ask, such as "best [your service] near [your neighborhood]". See whether you're mentioned and whether the details are right. If something's wrong, it usually traces back to an outdated listing or a gap on your website.

Every local SEO plan includes this kind of check, and the website guide covers what your site needs to be clear to people and machines.
