"How often should I post?" is the question I hear most from business owners. The honest answer: less often than you fear, but more consistently than you probably do now. Here's a rhythm that works for a busy local business.

A realistic weekly rhythm

For most local businesses, this is a strong, sustainable starting point on one or two platforms:

  • Three feed posts a week, spread out, for example Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday.
  • Stories on most days, quick and casual: today's special, a behind-the-scenes moment, a reminder.
  • One or two short videos a month to start, building up as you get comfortable.

It's far better to post three good things every week for a year than to post every day for a month and then disappear. When an account goes quiet, people wonder if you're still open.

Pick your platforms on purpose

You don't need to be everywhere. Choose based on where your customers already spend time:

  • Instagram for anything visual: food, beauty, hospitality, retail, fitness.
  • Facebook for local community, events, and customers who are a bit older.
  • TikTok if you're comfortable on camera and your business has personality to show.
  • LinkedIn if you serve other businesses or want to reach professionals.

Doing one platform well beats doing four badly. Add a second once the first is running smoothly.

Five content pillars that never run dry

Rotate through these and you'll always have something to post:

  1. Behind the scenes. Prep work, your workspace, how something is made.
  2. People. Introduce your team, your regulars (with permission) and yourself.
  3. Proof. Before-and-afters, finished projects, kind words from real customers.
  4. Helpful tips. Quick advice related to what you do. These get saved and shared.
  5. Offers and news. Specials, new items, events and seasonal changes. Keep these to about one in five posts.

Plan a month at a time

The secret to consistency is not deciding what to post every morning. Once a month, sit down for an hour and:

  • Note the dates that matter: holidays, local events, your own promotions.
  • Assign a pillar to each post slot.
  • Take a batch of photos and short clips in one go, when the light is good.
  • Write captions and schedule everything ahead.

Then your daily job is just replying to comments and messages, and adding a few Stories.

Reply like a neighbor

Social media is a conversation. Reply to comments and messages within a day, in a warm, human voice. A quick, friendly answer to "Are you open Sunday?" can turn into a sale the same afternoon.

Measure what matters

Likes feel nice, but they don't pay rent. Each month, look at the numbers tied to real business:

  • Messages and comments asking about prices, booking or availability
  • Taps to call, get directions or visit your website
  • Customers who mention they "saw you on Instagram"

Keep doing what brings those in, and quietly drop what doesn't.
