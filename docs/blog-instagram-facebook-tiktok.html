Instagram, Facebook or TikTok: where should a local business show up?

By Karla · November 22, 2026 · 2 min read

In this post
  1. Start with your customers, not the platform
  2. Instagram
  3. Facebook
  4. TikTok
  5. And don't forget Google
  6. A simple way to choose

You don't need to be everywhere. A local business does better showing up well in one or two places than posting half-heartedly on five. Here's how to choose.

Start with your customers, not the platform

Ask a few regulars where they spend time on their phones, and where they found you. Look at where people already tag you or message you. The best platform is the one your customers already open every day, and one you can keep up with.

Instagram

Good for: anything visual. Restaurants, cafés, salons, boutiques, hotels, real estate and anyone with a great before and after.

Instagram is a shop window. People look at your profile to judge whether you're their kind of place. A clean grid, short Reels and helpful Stories work together: Reels help new people find you, and Stories keep regulars close. Many people also message businesses there to ask questions or book.

Facebook

Good for: local services, family businesses, community-minded shops and customers who like to read reviews and details.

Facebook still works well for local communities, events and groups. Your page is a place for hours, updates, events and reviews, and plenty of customers still message businesses there. Posts can be shared from Instagram to save time, but reply to comments and messages on both.

TikTok

Good for: businesses with personality, process or a story: food being made, transformations, behind the scenes, a funny owner.

TikTok shows videos to people who don't follow you yet, so it can bring in new customers fast, but it rewards regular, natural video. If you or someone on your team enjoys being on camera, it's worth a try. If not, it's fine to skip it.

And don't forget Google

Strictly speaking it isn't social media, but your Google Business Profile is often where a customer decides. Photos, updates, reviews and replies there reach people who are ready to visit right now. If you only have time for one thing, make it that. See the 20-minute tune-up.

A simple way to choose

  • Very visual business: Instagram first, Facebook alongside.
  • Local service or trade: Google first, then Facebook.
  • Lots of personality or process to show: add TikTok and reuse the same videos as Instagram Reels.
  • Little time: one platform, done well, beats three done badly.
