A worldly eye

How to win customers who are just visiting

By Karla, founder of Digital Mondo · November 26, 2026 · 7 min read

  2. Make Google Maps your front door
  3. Some visitors plan on other apps entirely

Key takeaways
  • Visitors decide on their phones, often before they arrive.
  • Google Maps is the front door for most travelers, so make your profile work in any language.
  • Some visitors plan on other apps entirely. Learn which ones your guests use.
  • Small touches, like a translated menu or a WhatsApp number, win big.

Some of your best customers don't live nearby. Tourists, business travelers, students and families who just moved to town all find local businesses differently from your regulars. Having traveled widely myself, here's what I've learned about showing up for them.

Visitors decide on their phones

Someone in an unfamiliar city doesn't drive past your sign every day. They search, scroll and save places on their phone, often days before they arrive, then pull up that list when they're hungry, need a haircut or want something to do. If you're not easy to find and easy to understand on a phone, you don't make their list.

Make Google Maps your front door

For most international and domestic travelers, Google Maps is where the decision happens. Your Google Business Profile needs to work for someone who has never heard of you:

  • Photos that explain themselves. Show the entrance, the menu or price list, and what people actually get. Pictures cross language barriers.
  • Accurate hours, including holidays. Visitors can't come back tomorrow.
  • Clear services, menus and prices on the profile itself.
  • Attributes like "accepts credit cards," "wheelchair accessible" or "good for kids," which answer travelers' practical questions.
  • Reviews with replies. Visitors lean heavily on reviews because they have nothing else to go on.

Some visitors plan on other apps entirely

This is where a worldly eye helps. Not every traveler uses the same apps you do:

  • WhatsApp is how much of the world messages businesses. A WhatsApp number on your profile and website can be the easiest way for international visitors to ask a question or book.
  • Visitors from mainland China grow up without Google, Instagram or WhatsApp, which are blocked there, and many plan trips on RedNote (Xiaohongshu) and message on WeChat. If these visitors matter to you, a presence there can set you apart.
  • Travelers from Japan, Taiwan and Thailand often use LINE, and visitors from Korea often use KakaoTalk.
  • Tripadvisor and booking sites still matter for hotels, tours and restaurants in popular areas.

You don't need all of these. Look at who actually walks in, ask a few guests where they found you, and focus on the one or two channels that fit.

Small touches that win big

  • A translated menu or price list, even just in one or two languages your visitors speak most.
  • Simple English on your website and profile. Short sentences translate better, whether a visitor reads them directly or through a translation app.
  • Payment clarity: say which cards and mobile wallets you accept.
  • Directions from where visitors are staying: the nearest hotel cluster, transit stop or landmark.
  • A warm welcome in your posts for visitors, especially around big local events and travel seasons.

Plan around the calendar

Visitor traffic follows a rhythm: conferences, festivals, sports seasons, school breaks and international holidays such as Golden Week. Note the dates that bring people to your area and post about them a few weeks ahead, when travelers are planning.

Don't forget the new locals

People who just moved to town behave like visitors for their first few months. They're actively looking for a new café, dentist, gym and hairdresser. Being welcoming, easy to find and well reviewed is how you become their regular for years.
