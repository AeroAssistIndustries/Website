Reputation

How to get more Google reviews, the right way

By Karla, founder of Digital Terra · November 26, 2026 · 7 min read

In this guide
  1. Why reviews matter so much
  2. First, the rules (they matter more than ever)
  3. The simple system: ask everyone, at the right moment
  4. Reply to every review
  5. What about unfair or fake reviews?
  6. A 30-day plan
Key takeaways
  • Ask every customer, not just the happy ones, and never offer anything in return.
  • Make it one tap: a short review link and a QR card at the counter.
  • Reply to every review within two days, good or bad.
  • Fake, bought or incentivized reviews can cost you your profile and break federal rules.

Reviews are often the last thing someone reads before they call you, book you or walk in. The good news: you don't need tricks to get more of them. You need a habit. Here's the simple, rule-following system I set up for my clients.

Why reviews matter so much

When someone searches for a café, a salon or a plumber nearby, Google shows a short list of businesses with their star ratings right on the map. People compare those stars, read a few recent reviews, and decide in seconds. A business with plenty of fresh, genuine reviews, each with a thoughtful reply, simply looks more trustworthy than one with a handful of reviews from three years ago.

Reviews also help Google understand what you do. When customers mention "gluten-free pastries" or "same-day AC repair" in their own words, that's useful information about your business, written by the people you served.

First, the rules (they matter more than ever)

Review rules have tightened, and both Google and the U.S. government enforce them. Before asking for a single review, know what's off-limits:

  • No incentives. Google's policy doesn't allow offering discounts, free items or anything else in exchange for posting, changing or removing a review. That includes "10% off for an honest review."
  • No review gating. Asking only your happy customers, or sending unhappy ones to a private form instead of Google, isn't allowed. Ask everyone the same way.
  • No fake or bought reviews. The Federal Trade Commission's rule on consumer reviews, in effect since October 2024, bans buying, selling or writing fake reviews, and the FTC can seek large civil penalties from businesses that knowingly break it.
  • No undisclosed insider reviews. Reviews from employees, owners or close family need to make that connection clear, so it's simplest not to ask them at all.
  • Don't script the review. Ask for honest feedback. Don't ask for a specific star rating, tell people what to write, or ask them to name a particular staff member.

Breaking these rules can get reviews removed, new reviews paused or a profile restricted. None of that is worth a few extra stars.

The simple system: ask everyone, at the right moment

Most happy customers will gladly leave a review. They just need to be asked, at the right time, in a way that takes ten seconds.

1. Get your review link

In your Google Business Profile, look for the option to ask for reviews. It gives you a short link that opens the review box directly. Save it somewhere you can paste it fast.

2. Ask at the high point

The best moment is right after the customer feels the result: they love their haircut, the leak is fixed, the meal was great. A friendly, in-person ask works best: "If you have a minute, a Google review really helps a small business like mine."

3. Make it one tap

  • A small QR card at the counter, on the table or with the receipt.
  • A follow-up text or email within 24 hours, with the link.
  • Your email signature and receipts can carry the link too.

4. Use the same message for everyone

Follow-up text

Hi Maria, thanks for coming in today! If you have a minute, would you share your experience on Google? It really helps us. [your review link]

Send it to every customer, not just the ones who smiled the most. Consistency is what keeps you inside the rules.

Reply to every review

Replies are where you win the next customer. People read how you respond, especially to the critical reviews. Aim to reply within two days.

  • Positive reviews: thank them by name, mention something specific, and invite them back. Keep it short and warm.
  • Negative reviews: stay calm, thank them for the feedback, apologize for their experience without arguing about details, and offer to make it right offline with a phone number or email.
  • Never share private details about a customer in a reply.
Reply to a critical review

Hi James, thank you for telling us about this, and I'm sorry your order took so long on Saturday. That's not the experience we want for anyone. I'd love to make it right; please call me at [number] or email [address]. – Karla, Owner

What about unfair or fake reviews?

If a review breaks Google's policies, for example it's spam, offensive, or clearly not from a real customer, you can report it from your Business Profile. Reply calmly in the meantime; readers can usually tell when a review is unfair. Never pressure a customer to remove a review or threaten them. That can break both Google's policies and the FTC rule.

A 30-day plan

  1. Week 1: get your review link, print a QR card, and reply to every review you already have.
  2. Week 2: start asking every customer in person, and send the follow-up text the same day.
  3. Week 3: add the link to your receipts, email signature and website.
  4. Week 4: look at what's working, keep replying within two days, and make asking part of your routine.

Done consistently, this beats any shortcut, and it builds a profile that keeps working for you every month.
