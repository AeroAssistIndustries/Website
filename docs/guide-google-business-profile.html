The 20-minute Google Business Profile tune-up

Key takeaways
  • Your primary category matters more than almost anything else on the profile.
  • Use your real-world business name. Adding keywords can get a profile suspended.
  • Keep hours, holiday hours and your website link accurate.
  • Fresh photos and a short weekly post show Google and customers you're open and active.

For most local businesses, the Google Business Profile is the real homepage. It's what people see on Google Search and Maps before they ever reach your website. Here's how to tune it up in about twenty minutes, plus the mistakes that can get a profile suspended.

Why your profile matters

When someone searches "coffee near me" or "salon open now," Google shows a map with a few businesses. Your profile decides whether you appear there, and whether people tap "Call," "Directions" or "Website" once they see you. A complete, accurate, active profile gives you the best chance of being chosen.

1. Use your real business name

Google's guidelines say your profile name should match your real-world name, the one on your sign, website and receipts. Adding extra keywords or locations ("Bella's Salon | Best Haircuts Phoenix") isn't allowed and can lead to a suspension. Keep it exactly as customers know you.

2. Choose the right primary category

Your primary category is one of the strongest signals for which searches you show up in. Choose the most specific one that describes what you are, not everything you do: "Italian restaurant" rather than "Restaurant," "Hair salon" rather than "Beauty." Then add a few secondary categories for other things you genuinely offer. Don't add categories just as keywords.

3. Get the basics exactly right

  • Address or service area. Storefronts show an address. If you travel to customers and don't serve people at your location, use a service area instead.
  • Phone number. Use a local number that's answered during business hours.
  • Website. Link to your homepage, or to a page for that specific location if you have several.
  • Hours. Keep regular hours current and add holiday hours ahead of time. Nothing loses a customer faster than showing up to a closed door.

4. Write a helpful description

You have up to 750 characters. Use them to say who you are, what you offer and what makes you different, in plain language. Mention your neighborhood naturally. Leave out links, all-caps and sales claims like "best prices in town."

Example

Juniper Street Café is a neighborhood coffee shop serving locally roasted espresso, cold brew and fresh pastries baked each morning. We open early for commuters, have plenty of seating for remote work, and offer oat, almond and soy milk at no extra charge. Dogs are welcome on the patio.

5. Fill in services, products and attributes

Add your main services or menu items with short descriptions and prices where it makes sense. Then go through the attributes Google offers for your category, like outdoor seating, wheelchair access, online appointments or languages spoken. Each one helps the right people decide you're a fit.

6. Add real photos, and keep adding them

Photos help people picture the visit. Start with:

  • Your logo and a clean cover photo
  • The outside, so people recognize you from the street
  • The inside, your team at work and your best products or results

Add a few new photos every month. Real, well-lit phone photos beat stock images every time.

7. Post once a week

Profile updates show customers you're open and active. Share a seasonal special, a new product, an event or a quick tip, with a photo and a button like "Call" or "Learn more." One short update a week is plenty.

Be careful with big changes

Editing your name, address or categories over and over can trigger a review of your profile. Make the changes you need, make them accurately, and then leave them alone. Never create a second profile for the same location.
